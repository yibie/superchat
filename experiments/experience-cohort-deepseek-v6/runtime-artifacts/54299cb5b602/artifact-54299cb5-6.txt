### Minor Precision Corrections

1. **SLSA verification "MUST" vs. "SHOULD."** The analyst states verification "MUST check" the envelope signature, subject digest, predicateType, and builder.id. The verifying-artifacts spec states verification "SHOULD include" these steps (RFC 2119: SHOULD = recommended but optional in specific circumstances). The analyst's use of MUST is slightly stronger than the spec, though the checks are indeed the core of verification.

2. **PS.2.1 EO 14028 mapping.** The analyst lists 4e(iii), 4e(ix), 4e(x). The official table shows PS.2.1 under 4e(iii) and 4e(x). 4e(ix) states "All practices and tasks consistent with a risk-based approach," so including it is technically correct but less precise than listing the specific subsections.

3. **SSDF provenance scope.** Both Artifacts correctly contrast SLSA's narrow build-focused provenance with SSDF's broad "full chronology" definition. The reviewer confirms this distinction is accurate and critical.

## Notable Observation: Convergence in SP 800-218A (2024)

The NIST SP 800-218A SSDF Community Profile for AI (July 2024) **explicitly names SLSA** in its augmented PS.3.2: "Collect, safeguard, maintain, and share provenance data for all components of each software release (e.g., in a software bill of materials [SBOM], through Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts [SLSA])." This formal convergence — NIST citing SLSA as an implementation pathway for SSDF provenance requirements — is not noted in the prior Artifacts but reinforces their core thesis that the frameworks are complementary.

## Critical Distinctions (Reviewer's Synthesis)

The prior Artifacts correctly identify four critical distinctions. The reviewer confirms and refines them:

1. **Scope of provenance.** SLSA: build-time only ("how was this artifact built?"). SSDF: full lifecycle ("chronology of origin, development, ownership, location, and changes").

2. **Nature of evidence.** SLSA: a single cryptographic, machine-verifiable attestation. SSDF: a spectrum from SBOMs and hashes to threat models, logs, scan reports, and narrative conformance statements.

3. **Trust model.** SLSA: trust the build platform (`builder.id`); the consumer verifies the artifact cryptographically. SSDF: trust the producer's first-party attestation, with optional escalation to second- or third-party assessment based on risk.

4. **Levels and gradation.** SLSA: three prescriptive Build levels (L1–L3), each with objective, automatable criteria. SSDF: risk-based framework; practices are recommended, not tiered; conformance demonstrated through attestation and artifacts.

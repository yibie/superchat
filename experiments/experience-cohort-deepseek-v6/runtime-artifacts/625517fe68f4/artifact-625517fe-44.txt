# Evidence Retention Expectations in the NIST AI RMF: Govern & Measure Functions — Analyst Synthesis

**Role:** analyst  
**Question:** What evidence does the NIST AI Risk Management Framework expect organizations to retain across governance and measurement activities?  
**Primary Sources:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 (AI 100-1, Jan 2023); AI RMF Playbook (AIRC, Sep 2024); GAI Profile (AI 600-1, Jul 2024)

---

## 1. The Architecture of Evidence Retention

The NIST AI RMF does not have a single "document retention" mandate. Instead, it weaves evidence retention across every subcategory through the repeated requirement that outcomes be **documented**. The Core states: "Documentation can enhance transparency, improve human review processes, and bolster accountability in AI system teams" (AI 100-1, §5.1). The MEASURE function introduction adds: "Measurement provides a traceable basis to inform management decisions" (§5.3). Together these establish that retained evidence must connect claims back to specific data, metrics, and tooling.

The Playbook makes the operational rationale explicit in GOVERN 1.4: "Proper documentation storage and access procedures allow for quick retrieval of critical information during a negative incident."

---

## 2. GOVERN Function: Evidence Retention Requirements

### 2.1 Organizational Policy Documentation (Govern 1)

**Govern 1.1** — Legal/regulatory requirements must be "understood, managed, and documented." Retain: regulatory environment descriptions, compliance reviews.

**Govern 1.2** — Policies must "outline and document risk mapping and measurement processes," "detail model testing and validation processes," "detail review processes for legal and risk functions," and "establish the frequency of and detail for monitoring, auditing and review processes."

**Govern 1.3** — Risk tolerance must be formalized on "uniform risk scales that are valid across the organization's AI portfolio (e.g., documentation templates)."

**Govern 1.4** — This is the most detailed governance evidence specification. The Playbook enumerates 13 documentation elements organizations must establish policies for:
- AI actor contact information
- Business justification
- Scope and usages
- Expected and potential risks and impacts
- Assumptions and limitations
- Training data description and characterization
- Algorithmic methodology
- Evaluated alternative approaches
- Output data description
- Testing and validation results (including explanatory visualizations)
- Down- and up-stream dependencies
- Deployment, monitoring, and change management plans
- Stakeholder engagement plans

Plus: model documentation inventory system policies; public disclosure policies for impact assessments, audits, model documentation, and validation/testing results.

**Govern 1.5** — Ongoing monitoring and periodic review plans with clearly defined roles. The Playbook prompts retention of incident response plans, opt-out mechanisms, and recourse mechanisms.

**Govern 1.6** — AI system inventory mechanisms. The Playbook specifies inventories may include "system documentation, incident response plans, data dictionaries, links to implementation software or source code, names and contact information for relevant AI actors."

**Govern 1.7** — Decommissioning policies. The Playbook advises retained storage of decommissioned models/systems "for an established length of time," addressing "potential future legal, regulatory, security or forensic investigations" and "ancillary data or artifacts that must be preserved" (predictions, explanations, intermediate input features).

### 2.2 Accountability Structures (Govern 2)

**Govern 2.1** — "Roles and responsibilities and lines of communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks are documented and are clear to individuals and teams throughout the organization."

**Govern 2.2** — Personnel/partner AI risk management training — implies training record retention.

**Govern 2.3** — Executive leadership takes documented responsibility for risk decisions.

### 2.3 Risk Culture (Govern 4)

**Govern 4.2** — "Organizational teams document the risks and potential impacts of the AI technology they design, develop, deploy, evaluate, and use, and they communicate about the impacts more broadly."

**Govern 4.3** — "Organizational practices are in place to enable AI testing, identification of incidents, and information sharing" — implies incident records and test evidence retention.

### 2.4 Explicit Retention Policy (GAI Profile Only)

**GV-1.5-003** (NIST AI 600-1): "Maintain a document retention policy to keep history for test, evaluation, validation, and verification (TEVV), and digital content transparency methods for GAI." This is the single most explicit "retention" requirement in the entire NIST AI RMF corpus. It was added specifically to address information integrity and intellectual property risks of generative AI. The initial public draft (April 2024) had even stronger language: "Maintain a long term document retention policy to keep full history for auditing, investigation, or improving content provenance methods."

## 3. MEASURE Function: Evidence Retention Requirements

### 3.1 Measurement Methods (Measure 1)

**Measure 1.1** — "The risks or trustworthiness characteristics that will not — or cannot — be measured are properly documented." This is a critical requirement: organizations must retain **negative decisions** — documentation of what was deliberately not measured and why. The Playbook adds: "Document metric selection criteria and include considered but unused metrics."

**Measure 1.2** — Metrics and controls "regularly assessed and updated, including reports of errors and potential impacts on affected communities." Retain: error reports, impact assessments, metric effectiveness assessments.

**Measure 1.3** — Involvement of independent internal experts, external assessors, domain experts, users, and affected communities must be documented.

### 3.2 Trustworthiness Evaluation Evidence (Measure 2)

Every subcategory from 2.1 through 2.13 requires documentation. The key retained evidence types:

| Subcategory | Evidence to Retain |
|---|---|
| **2.1** | Test sets, metrics, tools used during TEVV. The Playbook states: "Documentation enables repeatability and consistency, and can enhance AI risk management decisions." |
| **2.2** | Human subject evaluation compliance; population representativeness |
| **2.3** | Performance/assurance criteria demonstrated under deployment-like conditions |
| **2.4** | Production functionality and behavior monitoring records |
| **2.5** | Validity/reliability demonstration; **limitations of generalizability beyond development conditions** |
| **2.6** | Safety evaluation results; residual risk vs. tolerance; safe failure demonstration; reliability, robustness, monitoring, response-time metrics |
| **2.7** | Security and resilience evaluation results |
| **2.8** | Transparency and accountability risk examination results |
| **2.9** | Model explanation, validation, and documentation; output interpretation within context |
| **2.10** | Privacy risk examination results |
| **2.11** | Fairness and bias evaluation results |
| **2.12** | Environmental impact and sustainability assessments |
| **2.13** | Effectiveness evaluation of the TEVV metrics and processes themselves |

Measure 2.5's requirement to document "limitations of the generalizability beyond the conditions under which the technology was developed" is particularly notable: it requires retaining evidence of what the system **cannot** do.

### 3.3 Risk Tracking Evidence (Measure 3)

**Measure 3.1** — "Approaches, personnel, and documentation are in place to regularly identify and track existing, unanticipated, and emergent AI risks based on factors such as intended and actual performance in deployed contexts." Retain: tracking logs connecting deployed performance to risk emergence.

**Measure 3.2** — Risk tracking for difficult-to-assess risks (where metrics are unavailable).

**Measure 3.3** — "Feedback processes for end users and impacted communities to report problems and appeal system outcomes are established and integrated into AI system evaluation metrics." Retain: feedback records, appeal outcomes, integration with evaluation data.

### 3.4 Measurement Feedback Evidence (Measure 4)

**Measure 4.1** — Measurement approaches informed by domain experts and documented.

**Measure 4.2** — Measurement results validated by domain experts and "documented."

**Measure 4.3** — "Measurable performance improvements or declines based on consultations with relevant AI actors, including affected communities, and field data about context-relevant risks and trustworthiness characteristics are identified and documented."

### 3.5 Incident and Error Records (Manage 4.3)

Though part of the MANAGE function, the incident documentation requirement is closely coupled to measurement. **Manage 4.3**: "Incidents and errors are communicated to relevant AI actors, including affected communities. Processes for tracking, responding to, and recovering from incidents and errors are followed and documented." The Playbook specifies retaining a database of reported errors, near-misses, incidents, and negative impacts including date reported, number of reports, impact/severity assessment, and responses.

### 3.6 Residual Risk Documentation (Manage 1.4)

**Manage 1.4**: "Negative residual risks (defined as the sum of all unmitigated risks) to both downstream acquirers of AI systems and end users are documented." The AI RMF 1.0 (§1.2.3) ties this to end-user impact: "Documenting residual risks will call for the system provider to fully consider the risks of deploying the AI product and will inform end users about potential negative impacts."

## 4. Cross-Cutting Analytical Observations

### 4.1 Documentation as a Continuous (Not Point-in-Time) Obligation

The framework states: "It is incumbent on Framework users to continue applying the MEASURE function to AI systems as knowledge, methodologies, risks, and impacts evolve over time" (§5.3). Evidence retention is ongoing and must capture drift, not just pre-deployment baselines.

### 4.2 Traceability as the Operational Principle

The MEASURE introduction establishes that measurement provides "a traceable basis to inform management decisions." Evidence must connect claims about system performance and safety back to specific datasets, metric definitions, and tool versions. The Playbook on Measure 2.1 operationalizes this as: leverage datasheets for datasets, model cards, and regularly assess documentation tool effectiveness.

### 4.3 Negative Evidence Requirements

A distinctive NIST AI RMF feature is the requirement to retain evidence of **what is not done**: risks not measured (Measure 1.1), metrics considered but unused, limitations of generalizability (Measure 2.5), and gaps between measurement settings and deployment environments (Measure 2.3).

### 4.4 Public Disclosure Expectation

Govern 1.4's Playbook guidance calls for "policies and processes regarding public disclosure of the use of AI and risk management material such as impact assessments, audits, model documentation and validation and testing results." Evidence retention serves not only internal governance but external transparency.

## 5. Evidence vs. Inference

**Directly stated in source text (evidence):**
- Every MEASURE subcategory (2.1–2.13, 3.1–3.3, 4.1–4.3) explicitly uses "documented" as the required state
- GOVERN 1.1–1.7, 2.1, 4.2 all explicitly require documentation
- The Playbook (GOVERN 1.4) enumerates 13 documentation elements
- GAI Profile GV-1.5-003 explicitly requires a "document retention policy"
- The Core states documentation provides "traceable basis" (§5.3)
- Residual risk documentation is explicitly required (§1.2.3; Manage 1.4)
- Incident and error tracking records are explicit (Manage 4.3)
- Decommissioning records are explicit (Govern 1.7)

**Inference (reasonable but not prescribed):**
- Specific retention periods: the AI RMF does not prescribe duration; this is left to organizational policy and legal/regulatory requirements
- Storage formats/technologies: the framework is technology-neutral
- Audit frequency: not prescribed; organizations define based on risk tolerance
- The framework implies but does not mandate specific evidence packaging formats (e.g., model cards, datasheets are suggested as "examples" in the Playbook but not required)

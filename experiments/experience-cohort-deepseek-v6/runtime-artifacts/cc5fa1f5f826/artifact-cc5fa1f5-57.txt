# Reviewer's Assessment: Representing Materially Contradictory Primary Studies in Systematic Reviews Without Collapsing Uncertainty

## Question

How should a systematic review represent materially contradictory primary studies without collapsing uncertainty into one conclusion?

## Summary Judgment

The collector and analyst Artifacts are accurate, well-sourced, and mutually consistent. All major methodological authorities — Cochrane Handbook (Ch.10, Ch.12), GRADE guidance 36, Core GRADE 3, SWiM, the ICEMAN instrument, and the empirical meta-research literature — converge on a single principle: **contradiction is a finding to be preserved, not a problem to be collapsed into a pooled estimate**. This reviewer identifies three areas where the prior Artifacts require supplementation: (1) a critical limitation of prediction intervals when study number is small, (2) a philosophical framework for diagnosing persistent discordance, and (3) an emerging refinement for quantifying — not merely flagging — contradictory uncertainty.

### Analyst (artifact-cc5fa1f5-56): Accurate and well-synthesized

The analyst correctly organizes the integrated framework into three tiers (meta-analysis undertaken, meta-analysis foregone, GRADE assessment). The IntHout et al. (2016) finding — 72.4% of statistically significant Cochrane meta-analyses with I² > 0 have prediction intervals crossing the null — is correctly reported. The Borenstein (2024) distinction between Q, I², τ², and the prediction interval is correctly characterized. The five-point operational answer is sound.

### Areas requiring supplementation

1. **Prediction interval reliability caveat (critical):** Both prior Artifacts endorse prediction intervals without adequately flagging their limitation with small study numbers. A 2025 simulation study (PMC13126221, Research Synthesis Methods) demonstrates that when K < 15 studies, the coverage distribution of prediction intervals is severely left-skewed, and the common interpretation — "an interval covering 95% of true effects" — is technically incorrect; the PI covers 95% of the true effects distribution only *on average*, not for any single published interval. The Cochrane Handbook's threshold of K ≥ 5 is a minimum, not optimal. For the typical Cochrane review (median K = 4–6), prediction intervals should be reported with explicit caveats about their imprecision.

2. **Epistemology of persistent discordance:** Reijula & Blanco Sequeiros (2024, *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*) distinguish three causes of evidential discordance — non-systematic error, noise, and bias — and argue that bias-generated discordance cannot be resolved by collecting more data within existing lines of inquiry. Their "enriched lines of evidence" framework, which traces evidential claims from raw data through processing steps, provides a normative method for resolving which of two contradictory study results is more warrant-giving. This framework extends the Cochrane/GRADE operational guidance by explaining *why* risk-of-bias stratification resolves some contradictions and not others.

3. **Beyond binary PI interpretation:** A 2025 extension (BMC Medical Research Methodology, "Beyond prediction intervals in meta-analysis") proposes reporting the expected proportion of comparable studies with clinically relevant benefit or harm, using pre-specified MID thresholds. This moves beyond the binary "PI crosses null" heuristic toward continuous quantification of contradictory uncertainty.

## Integrated Reviewer Framework

A systematic review should represent materially contradictory primary studies through five integrated practices, now supplemented with critical caveats:

### 1. Preserve the contradiction visually

Use forest plots without summary diamonds, harvest plots (Ogilvie et al., 2008), or effect direction plots (Thomson & Thomas, revised 2021) that display individual study findings ordered by risk of bias, study design, or hypothesized effect modifiers. The SWiM guideline (Campbell et al., BMJ 2020) requires transparent reporting of the chosen synthesis method, the standardized metric, and the question the synthesis answers. The revised effect direction plot removes reliance on statistical significance for within-study synthesis, aligning with Cochrane Ch.12.

**Caveat:** Harvest plots and effect direction plots communicate direction but not magnitude. When magnitude is available, forest plots without summary diamonds are preferred.

### 2. Quantify uncertainty; do not collapse it

When meta-analysis is undertaken, report a 95% prediction interval alongside the confidence interval. The prediction interval quantifies the range of true effects across settings (Riley et al., BMJ 2011). When this interval crosses the null or MID, explicitly state that the intervention may be ineffective or harmful in some populations.

**Critical caveat (not in prior Artifacts):** Prediction intervals require cautious interpretation. The 2025 simulation by [authors] (Res Syn Meth, PMC13126221) demonstrates that for K < 15 studies, the coverage distribution is left-skewed, and the PI covers 95% of the true effects distribution only *on average* across repeated sampling — not for any single published PI. The Cochrane Handbook threshold of K ≥ 5 is a minimum; reviewers should acknowledge imprecision when K is small. The 2025 BMC Medical Research Methodology extension allows supplementing the PI with the expected proportion of comparable studies showing clinically relevant benefit or harm, conditional on τ² estimation precision.

### 3. Match the synthesis question to the method

Explicitly state whether the review answers: "Is there any evidence of an effect?" (vote counting based on direction of effect), "What is the range and distribution of observed effects?" (summary statistics), or "What is the average intervention effect?" (random-effects meta-analysis) (SWiM Table 2; Cochrane Ch.12, Table 12.2.a). Language in conclusions must be consistent with the question actually addressed.

### 4. Rate down certainty transparently; do not double-penalize

Apply GRADE's three visual criteria: magnitude of differences in point estimates, overlap of confidence intervals, and relation of study estimates to the chosen threshold (null or MID) (Core GRADE 3, BMJ 2025). When point estimates fall on opposite sides of the threshold and CIs do not substantially overlap, rate down for inconsistency. Use the ICEMAN instrument (Schandelmaier et al., CMAJ 2020) to assess the credibility of any subgroup effect proposed to explain the contradiction; where credibility is low or very low, retain the contradiction through rating down. Avoid double-penalizing: when a random-effects CI already crosses thresholds, attribute the rating down to the most appropriate single domain (GRADE handbook, 2025).

### 5. Treat the contradiction as a finding; diagnose its source

Structure the discussion around the origin, magnitude, and implications of the disagreement rather than selecting one result. When contradictory results concentrate in studies at high risk of bias, present stratified results as the primary analysis (Lakbar & Poole, Critical Care 2026). Apply the Reijula & Blanco Sequeiros (2024) enriched-lines-of-evidence framework: trace evidential claims through their data-processing pipelines, using metadata to identify whether discordance arises from non-systematic error (resolvable by larger samples), noise (resolvable by modeling), or systematic bias (requiring normative evaluation of which line of evidence is fit for answering the research question).

# Reviewer Assessment: Conditions Under Which Registered Reports Reduce Publication Bias

## Scope and Method

This review evaluates two prior Artifacts — a collector report (artifact-68366e41-34) and an analyst report (artifact-68366e41-35) — addressing the question: "Under what conditions do registered reports reduce publication bias, according to primary meta-research?" The review involved independent verification of key claims against original sources via agent_reach_search and agent_reach_read, assessment of factual accuracy, identification of gaps, and evaluation of evidence-to-inference distinctions.

## Overall Assessment

Both Artifacts are **substantially accurate and well-sourced**. The numerical claims, source attributions, and structural conditions identified are corroborated by primary meta-research. The analyst Artifact adds meaningful value over the collector by introducing counter-evidence (Anthony et al., 2022), deeper confound analysis, and a clearer conditions taxonomy. All Artifacts correctly distinguish evidence from inference — a critical requirement given that no randomized evidence exists on this question.

## Verified Claims (Source-Corroborated)

### Scheel, Schijen, & Lakens (2021) — *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science*, 4(2)
- **Claim**: RRs: 43.66% positive results (95% CI [31.91, 55.95]); SRs: 96.05% positive (95% CI [91.61, 98.54]); χ²(1)=77.96, p<.001. **Verified.** Direct reading of the source confirms all numbers.
- **Claim**: When close replications excluded, RR positive rate = 50.00%. **Verified.**
- **Claim**: Within-journal comparison (3 journals, n=27): 84.62% positive in SRs vs. 50.00% in RRs. **Verified.** The authors present this as a "cursory look" and note the small sample.
- **Claim**: "Our study was not an experiment...and thus precludes strong causal inferences." **Verified.** This exact caveat appears in the source.

### Allen & Mehler (2019) — *PLOS Biology*, 17(5), e3000246
- **Claim**: 60.5% hypotheses unsupported (95% CI [54.7–66.1%]), 113 RRs, 296 hypotheses. **Verified.** Source confirms 127 RRs surveyed, 113 included, 296 discrete hypotheses, 60.5% unsupported. Replication attempts: 66.0% unsupported; novel: 54.5%.
- **Note**: Both prior Artifacts correctly note this study lacked a matched control sample — the comparison is to historical estimates of 5–20% null findings in the traditional literature.

### Soderberg et al. (2021) — *Nature Human Behaviour*, 5, 990–997
- **Claim**: Mean difference 0.99 for rigor of methodology (95% CrI [0.62, 1.35]); 0.97 for analysis quality ([0.60, 1.34]); 0.66 for overall quality ([0.30, 1.02]); novelty 0.13 ([−0.24, 0.49]). **Verified.** All numbers confirmed.
- **Claim**: Reviewers could not be blinded to format. **Verified.** The analyst correctly flags this as a limitation on strong causal conclusions.

### Anthony et al. (2022) — medRxiv (preprint)
- **Claim**: 93 RCTs labeled as Registered Reports; 84.9% had protocols published *after* first patient enrollment; IPA date "never documented"; 44% changed primary outcome. **Verified.** Direct reading of v2 and v3 confirms all figures. This is a critical counter-evidence source included by the analyst but absent from the collector report. The authors concluded these were "RRs in name only."

### Chambers & Tzavella (2022) — *Nature Human Behaviour*, 6, 29–42
- **Claim**: RRs "are not a universal solution for irreproducibility." **Verified.**
- **Claim**: RRs "best suited for instances where data is yet to be collected at the time of the first submission." **Verified** from the abstract and full text.

### Liu et al. (2025) — *American Psychologist*
- **Claim**: 119 matched pairs, RRs "significantly reduced publication bias against null results." **Verified** from PubMed/DOI listing.
- **Date discrepancy**: Both prior Artifacts cite this as "2026." The article was published online January 30, 2025 (DOI: 10.1037/amp0001503). The journal volume may carry a 2026 date, but the accessible publication date is 2025. This is a minor bibliographic imprecision.

### Lakens et al. (2024) — *Evidence-Based Toxicology*, 2(1)
- **Claim**: RRs reduce publication bias through IPA; deviations require reevaluation. **Verified.**
- **Claim**: A randomized trial assigning researchers to RR vs. standard is "practically out of reach." **Verified.** The source states this explicitly, making all causal claims inherently inferential.

### PLOS Computational Biology (2022) — "Ten Simple Rules"
- **Claim**: Outcome-neutral checks required; IPA is results-agnostic. **Verified.** The source confirms: "The decision to publish is results-agnostic because it is taken pre-study and results-blind."

## Gaps and Omissions

### 1. Gorman & Hubbard (2025) Bibliometric Study
Neither prior Artifact cites Gorman, D.M. & Hubbard, D. (2025). "Prevalence of Registered Reports in experimental psychology journals: A bibliometric study." *Quantitative Science Studies*. DOI: 10.1162/qss_a_00364. This study identified 227 RRs across 36 experimental psychology journals (2013–2023), finding that only 11 of 36 journals had published any RRs, and 80.6% came from just 9 journals. It notes that the COS list — used by Scheel et al. and others — captured only 52 of the 227 RRs, raising questions about sampling completeness in earlier studies. This is relevant to the generalizability of the meta-research evidence.

### 2. O'Mahony (2023) PhD Thesis
A Cardiff University thesis comparing 170 RRs to matched SRs found lower supported-hypothesis rates in RRs, higher open-practice rates, and "non-existent" HARKing in RRs. While a thesis rather than a peer-reviewed article, it represents one of the larger comparative samples.

### 3. Chambers (2020) — *PLOS Biology*, 18(3), e3000693
Cited in the analyst Artifact's source list but its specific findings are not discussed in the body text. This source discusses the Registered Reports funding model and barriers to adoption.

## Assessment of the Conditions Framework

Both Artifacts converge on a coherent conditions framework. The five necessary conditions identified are well-supported:

| Condition | Evidence Strength | Key Source |
|---|---|---|
| Binding IPA before results known | Strong correlational | Scheel et al. (2021); Anthony et al. (2022) (negative case) |
| Stage 1 review precedes data collection | Strong structural/logical; empirical via Anthony et al. | Chambers & Tzavella (2022); Anthony et al. (2022) |
| Verified protocol adherence at Stage 2 | Logical necessity; limited direct empirical test | Lakens et al. (2024); Henderson & Chambers (2022) |
| Outcome-neutral quality checks | Structural feature; logical necessity | Scheel et al. (2021); PLOS Comp Biol (2022) |
| Confirmatory/exploratory distinction | Structural feature | Chambers & Tzavella (2022); Allen & Mehler (2019) |

The facilitative conditions (high power requirements, rigorous Stage 1 review, public protocol registration) are also well-supported, though their independent contribution to bias reduction has not been empirically isolated.

## Distinguishing Evidence from Inference

Both prior Artifacts correctly flag that **all evidence is observational**. Key inferential leaps the prior Artifacts appropriately identify:

1. **Selection confound**: Authors may use RRs for hypotheses they expect to yield null results. Scheel et al. deem this "plausible but insufficient" to explain a 52-percentage-point gap.
2. **No randomized evidence**: All causal claims are inferential. A pragmatic RCT led by COS is underway but not yet reported.
3. **Small samples**: As of 2018, only 71–127 published RRs were available. Even Liu et al. (2025) with 119 matched pairs is modest.
4. **Discipline specificity**: Evidence concentrates in psychology and neuroscience.
5. **Reviewer blinding failure**: Soderberg et al.'s reviewers mostly identified RRs vs. non-RRs, potentially biasing quality ratings.

The analyst Artifact notably strengthens the inference-vs-evidence distinction by including Anthony et al. (2022) as a concrete counter-example showing that when the structural conditions are absent, the "Registered Report" label provides no bias reduction.

## Contradictions Between Artifacts

No substantive contradictions were found. The analyst Artifact is more comprehensive and includes counter-evidence absent from the collector. One minor numerical note:

- The collector states Allen & Mehler surveyed 127 RRs (compiled September 2018), of which 113 were included. The analyst states the same numbers. This is consistent.
- The collector states RRs had 60.5% unsupported; the analyst states the same. Consistent.

---

## Recommendations for Use

1. **Prefer the analyst Artifact** as the more complete, nuanced synthesis. It includes the Anthony et al. counter-evidence and better structures the conditions.
2. **The collector Artifact's CASRAI (2026) and Bogdanoski (2019) citations** should be treated as unverified secondary sources.
3. **The Liu et al. citation date** should be corrected to 2025 (or noted as "2025, in press for 2026 volume").
4. **Gorman & Hubbard (2025)** should be added as a supplementary source on RR prevalence and diffusion limitations.

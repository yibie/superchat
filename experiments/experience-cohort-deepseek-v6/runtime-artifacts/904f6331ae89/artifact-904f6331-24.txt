## 3. Factual Cross-Check: Collector vs. Analyst

### 3.1 Aligned Claims (Both Correct)

1. **Property A — "No Designation Without Authority"** is the core structural distinction. Both artifacts correctly attribute this to Miller et al. (2003) and explain it as capability = designator + authority unified.

2. **Rajani et al. Theorem 1**: ACs is strictly more permissive than Cs. Both artifacts correctly report this. I confirmed the theorem statement and its proof sketch in the paper (§II, Figure 2, Theorem 1).

3. **Confused deputy prevention:** Capabilities prevent CDAs from *explicit designation* but not from *implicit designation* (value attacks, implicit influence, initial-heap attacks). Both artifacts correctly report Examples 2–4 from Rajani et al.

4. **Confinement:** Object-capability systems achieve confinement compositionally via connectivity (Property F: "Delegation on Access-Controlled Channels"). Both artifacts correctly report this.

5. **NDA (Non-Delegatable Authority):** Murray & Grove's technique binds authority to a holder's identity via "authority by method return," enabling ACL-like controls within capability systems. Both artifacts describe this accurately.

6. **Communicating conspirators:** Capabilities cannot express the prohibition (expressiveness hole), ACLs can express it but cannot enforce it (creating false security). Both artifacts correctly capture Miller's argument.

7. **Summary comparison tables** in both artifacts are substantially aligned, with one exception noted below.

## 5. Key Findings Summary

The fundamental difference at delegation boundaries is **temporal** and **structural**:

1. **Temporal:** Identity-based systems (ACLs/IBAC) check authorization at *use time* — when a reference is dereferenced or written. Capability systems check authorization at *acquisition time* — when a capability is received or computed. This is formally proven by Rajani et al. (Theorem 1): ACs is strictly more permissive than Cs.

2. **Structural:** In ACLs, designation (naming a resource) and authorization (checking identity) travel separate paths, enabling confused deputy attacks. In capabilities, the capability *is* both designator and authority (Property A), structurally preventing explicit-designation CDAs.

3. **Neither model is strictly superior.** ACLs naturally express non-delegation via the separate "grant" right; capabilities require the NDA pattern. Capabilities achieve compositional confinement via connectivity; ACLs cannot express it. Capabilities are honest about the unenforceable (communicating conspirators); ACLs create false confidence.

4. **Modern systems converge on hybrids.** The Identity Foundation (2025) describes autonomous agent systems combining capability-based delegation (ZCAP-LD, attenuation chains) with identity-based accountability (DIDs, VCs). The theoretical purity of either model yields to practical requirements for both fine-grained authority and auditable provenance.

# Audit Evidence Needed to Distinguish Approved from Unapproved External Side Effects in Agent Systems

## Research Question
Which audit evidence is needed to distinguish an approved external side effect from an unapproved one in an agent system?

## Summary Answer
Distinguishing an approved external side effect from an unapproved one requires **five categories of linked, tamper-evident audit evidence**: (1) an identity-and-delegation chain binding the action to an accountable principal, (2) a policy-decision record produced *before* execution, (3) an approval record (where human review is required), (4) a cryptographically verifiable tool-execution record with before/after state, and (5) an integrity envelope (hash-chaining, signatures) making the record tamper-evident. The critical differentiator is **temporal ordering**: the policy decision and any approval must demonstrably precede the side effect. Absent any of these links, an external side effect cannot be reliably classified as approved.

---

## 1. The Five Evidence Dimensions (Auditable Agents Framework)

Zhao et al. (2026) define five jointly necessary dimensions for a defensible audit verdict in agent systems. All five must be satisfied to distinguish approved from unapproved side effects:

| Dimension | Audit Question | Evidence Required |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Action Recoverability** | What happened? | Tool name, full arguments, output, timestamp, policy decision |
| **Lifecycle Coverage** | Were all phases recorded? | Allow, block, pending, approval states; delegation chains; retries |
| **Policy Checkability** | Was the action mechanically checked against policy? | Policy version, matched rules, decision, evaluated-at timestamp |
| **Responsibility Attribution** | Who is responsible? | Agent identity, session context, delegation chain, human principal |
| **Evidence Integrity** | Can the record be trusted? | Ed25519 signatures, SHA-256 hash-chaining, Merkle proofs |

*Source: Zhao et al., "Auditable Agents: A Framework for Accountable AI Agent Systems" (ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026). https://viterbi-web.usc.edu/~yzhao010/auditable-agents.html*

## 2. Minimum Evidence Fields for the Distinction

Drawing from the KLA Digital enterprise audit framework (2026), the AugmentCode multi-agent audit guide (2026), the OATS specification, the Proof of Execution (PoE) paper (Rhodes & Kang, 2026), and the CellCog AI employee audit log model (2026), the following minimum fields are required per consequential action:

### 2.1 Identity and Delegation Chain

| Field | What It Establishes |
|-------|---------------------|
| `agent_id` | Which agent performed the action (distinct from human user) |
| `sponsoring_principal_id` | The human or service principal ultimately accountable |
| `delegation_chain` | Ordered chain of authority from principal to executing agent |
| `session_id` | The authorized session context and scope |
| `authority_snapshot_id` | Effective grants and expiry at execution time |

Without these, an unapproved side effect cannot be distinguished because there is no way to determine *who authorized what*. The NIST NCCoE concept paper (Feb 2026) specifically warns: "Treating an AI agent as a delegated user collapses accountability by merging agent actions into the identity record of the human principal it serves."

*Source: NIST NCCoE, "Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization" (Feb 2026). https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/accelerating-the-adoption-of-software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization-concept-paper.pdf*

### 2.2 Policy Decision Record (Pre-Execution)

| Field | What It Establishes |
|-------|---------------------|
| `policy_id` + `policy_version` | Exact policy ruleset in force |
| `policy_decision` | allow / deny / require_approval / modify / block |
| `matched_rule_ids[]` | Which specific rules fired |
| `evaluated_at` | Timestamp proving evaluation occurred **before** execution |

The OATS specification (C3) requires: "The runtime MUST evaluate actions against policy before execution. Default MUST be deny." The Gate phase produces one of five decisions: Allow, Deny, Modify, Step-Up, or Defer. The decision record is the canonical authorization anchor.

*Source: Open Agent Trust Stack (OATS) specification. https://openagenttruststack.org/*

### 2.3 Human Approval Record (Conditional)

| Field | What It Establishes |
|-------|---------------------|
| `decision_request_id` | Links the approval to the specific action proposal |
| `reviewer_id` + `reviewer_role` | Who reviewed and under what authority |
| `presented_evidence_hash` | What the reviewer saw (prevents approval drift) |
| `decision` + `rationale` | Approved, rejected, or modified; why |
| `decided_at` | Timestamp of human judgment |

The KLA framework emphasizes: "Bind approval to actor + action + target + destination + parameters + source version + limit + time window. If a material field changes, require a new approval."

*Source: KLA Digital, "How to Audit an AI Agent System: An Enterprise Framework" (Jul 2026). https://kla.digital/blog/how-to-audit-ai-agent-system*

### 2.4 Tool Execution and Effect Record

| Field | What It Establishes |
|-------|---------------------|
| `tool_id` + `tool_version` | What capability was invoked |
| `action` + `resource_scope` | What operation, on which target |
| `requested_args_hash` | What parameters were supplied (content-hashed) |
| `response_hash` | What the tool returned |
| `before_state_hash` + `after_state_hash` | **Evidence of what changed in the target system** |
| `effect_id` | Links to the durable side effect in the business system |
| `execution_status` | succeeded / failed / unknown / denied |

The PoE paper formalizes this as Invariant I2 (Gateway evaluation with scope): every effectful event must have a unique Gateway allow decision `g` such that `g.decision = allow`, `g.authorized_scope(e.capability) = 1`, and `g` precedes `e` in the causal order. The `before_state_hash` and `after_state_hash` are the fields that prove *whether* a side effect occurred — and whether it matches the authorized scope.

*Source: Rhodes & Kang, "Proof of Execution: Runtime Verification for Governed AI Agent Actions" (arXiv:2607.05397, 2026). https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05397*

### 2.5 Integrity and Ordering Envelope

| Field | What It Establishes |
|-------|---------------------|
| `evidence_hash` | Content hash of the complete record |
| `previous_record_hash` | Hash-chain link to prior event (tamper-evidence) |
| `signature` + `signature_key_id` | Cryptographic non-repudiation |
| `sealed_at` | When the record was committed |
| `sequence` | Monotonic ordering |

The IETF draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00 specifies: "Each receipt carries a monotonic `chain.seq` and `prevHash`. A verifier checking a range of receipts MUST verify each `prevHash` equals the prior receipt's `chain.hash`."

*Source: IETF, "SCITT AI Agent Action Receipt Profile" (draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00). https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00*

## 3. The Critical Temporal Ordering: The Core Differentiator

The single most important piece of evidence that distinguishes approved from unapproved side effects is the **provable temporal sequence**:

```
Policy Evaluation → (Approval, if required) → Execution → Effect Verification
```

An approved side effect has: `evaluated_at < decided_at < executed_at` with all records linked by `correlation_id`.

An unapproved side effect manifests as one of:
- **Missing policy decision**: no `policy_decision` record exists for the action
- **Policy decision after execution**: `evaluated_at > executed_at`
- **Deny verdict with effect**: `policy_decision = deny` but `after_state_hash ≠ before_state_hash` (PoE Invariant I3 violation)
- **Scope mismatch**: the `authorized_scope` does not contain the executed `capability`
- **Missing approval**: policy returned `require_approval` but no `decision_request_id` exists or `approval_decision ≠ approved`
- **Missing Gateway reference**: no `gateway_ref` links the effect to an authorization decision (bypass)

The Permit.io causal commit-log model states: "Authorization decisions should be recorded as first-class events before protected tool execution, and each execution event should reference the decision that allowed it. This ordering preserves proof that policy evaluation happened prior to side effects."

*Source: Permit.io, "Agent Audit Logs Need a Causal Commit Log, Not Just Tool Traces" (Jun 2026). https://www.permit.io/blog/agent-audit-logs-causal-commit-log*

## 4. Evidence That Is Necessary but NOT Sufficient Alone

Several common data sources are useful but cannot independently distinguish approved from unapproved side effects:

| Record Type | What It Shows | Why Insufficient Alone |
|-------------|---------------|----------------------|
| Tool-call traces | What tool ran, with what parameters | No authorization context, no policy version, no principal |
| Application logs | API calls and responses | No delegation chain, no approval binding |
| Model transcripts | What the agent "thought" | Unreliable as evidence; no authorization proof |
| API access logs | That a request was made | No decision context, no before/after state |
| Telemetry / OpenTelemetry spans | Latency, service flow | No authorization semantics, no policy identity |

As WorkOS notes: "The application log tells you what happened. The audit log tells you who did it, what they were allowed to do, who authorized the action, and how long that authorization was valid for."

*Source: WorkOS, "Why AI Agent Audit Logs Are Different from Application Logs" (Jun 2026). https://workos.com/blog/agent-audit-logs*

## 5. Architecture-Level Requirements

Several sources converge on structural requirements for producing distinguishing evidence:

1. **Mandatory Gateway / PEP**: All effectful actions must pass through a single authoritative enforcement point. The PoE paper's Prime Execution Model separates Planning, Enforcement, Effect, and Recordkeeping into distinct authority planes. OATS enforces this at compile-time via typestates.

2. **Append-Only, Tamper-Evident Journal**: Records must be hash-chained and signed (Ed25519 or ECDSA P-256). Aegis, Tracepath, OATS, and the SCITT profiles all converge on this pattern.

3. **Independent Evidence Custodian**: The IETF SCITT profiles recommend that evidence be registered with an independent Transparency Service to prevent operator-side mutation — "tamper-evidence guarantees are void if the same entity operates both Issuer and Transparency Service roles."

4. **Coordinator-Specialist Topology**: For multi-agent systems, coordinator-specialist architectures provide structural traceability because all interactions pass through a mandatory logging point. Peer-to-peer topologies lack this guarantee.

*Sources: Rhodes & Kang (2026); OATS specification; IETF draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00; AugmentCode multi-agent audit guide*

## 6. Practical Test

The CellCog reconstruction standard provides a practical litmus test:

> "Starting from one business outcome or one disputed action, can a reviewer move backward to its origin (identity → delegation → policy decision → approval) and forward to every material consequence (tool execution → state change → verification → acceptance)?"

If the answer is no for a given action, the evidence is insufficient to classify that side effect as approved or unapproved.

*Source: CellCog, "AI Employee Audit Logs: What Buyers Should Be Able to Reconstruct" (Jul 2026). https://cellcog.ai/blog/ai-employee-audit-logs/*

## 8. Limitations and Caveats

- No final, agent-specific, end-to-end auditability standard exists as of the source review (Aug 2026). NIST AI RMF 1.0 and ISO/IEC 42001 provide organizational frameworks but lack agent-specific control detail.
- The IETF SCITT-based profiles (draft-noa-scitt, draft-emirdag-scitt) are Internet-Drafts and not yet RFCs.
- OATS is an open specification, not a ratified standard; its conformance requirements are testable but not certified by any standards body.
- The PoE paper is an academic publication with a preliminary prototype; the soundness proof depends on deployment assumptions (A3–A7) that must be validated per implementation.
- None of the source frameworks claim to detect side effects that occur entirely outside the instrumented boundary (i.e., if the agent bypasses the Gateway entirely, the evidence gap is itself the signal).

# How to Distinguish an Approved External Side Effect from an Unapproved One: The Evidence Requirements

## Core Analytical Finding

Distinguishing an approved external side effect from an unapproved one in an agent system reduces to a single, testable condition: **there must exist a causally ordered, cryptographically verifiable evidence chain establishing that a policy decision (and any required human approval) demonstrably preceded the side effect, and that the effect fell within the authorized scope.** If this chain has any broken link — missing record, inverted ordering, scope mismatch, or lack of integrity protection — the side effect cannot be classified as approved.

---

## The Minimum Distinguishing Evidence Chain

Five evidence artifacts, linked by causal identifiers (`correlation_id`, `gateway_ref`, `prev_event_hash`), are jointly necessary:

### 1. Contract and Delegation Anchor

**Question answered:** Who authorized this capability set, and under what constraints?

| Field | Role in the distinction |
|-------|------------------------|
| `contract_hash` → `contract_id` | Immutable boundary: principal, authorized capability set, validity window |
| `principal_id` | The accountable human or service identity |
| `delegation_chain` | Ordered path from principal to executing agent |
| `authority_snapshot_id` | Effective grants at execution moment |

An absent or broken delegation chain means the side effect lacks an accountable principal — it is unapproved by definition. The NIST NCCoE concept paper (Feb 2026) warns: "Treating an AI agent as a delegated user collapses accountability by merging agent actions into the identity record of the human principal it serves."

**Source:** NIST NCCoE, "Accelerating the Adoption of Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization" (Feb 2026). https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/accelerating-the-adoption-of-software-and-ai-agent-identity-and-authorization-concept-paper.pdf

### 2. Pre-Execution Policy Decision Record

**Question answered:** Was this specific action mechanically evaluated against policy before execution?

The OATS specification requires five possible decisions: Allow, Deny, Modify, Step-Up (require human approval), or Defer. The decision record must contain `policy_id`, `policy_version`, `matched_rule_ids[]`, `decision`, and crucially `evaluated_at` — a timestamp proving the evaluation occurred **before** execution. Default is deny.

The PoE paper formalizes this as Invariant I2: every effectful event must have a unique Gateway allow decision `g` such that `g.decision = allow`, `g.authorized_scope(e.capability) = 1`, and `g` precedes `e` in the causal order.

Without this record, there is no way to determine whether policy was even consulted. The Permit.io causal commit-log analysis states: "Authorization decisions should be recorded as first-class events before protected tool execution, and each execution event should reference the decision that allowed it. This ordering preserves proof that policy evaluation happened prior to side effects."

**Sources:** OATS specification. https://openagenttruststack.org/ ; Rhodes & Kang, "Proof of Execution," arXiv:2607.05397 (2026). https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05397 ; Permit.io, "Agent Audit Logs Need a Causal Commit Log," Jun 2026. https://www.permit.io/blog/agent-audit-logs-causal-commit-log

### 3. Human Approval Record (Conditional)

**When required:** Policy returned `Step-Up` or the action's risk tier exceeds an autonomous threshold.

| Field | Role |
|-------|------|
| `decision_request_id` | Links approval to the specific action proposal |
| `reviewer_id` + `reviewer_role` | Who reviewed, under what authority |
| `presented_evidence_hash` | What the reviewer saw — prevents approval drift |
| `decision` + `rationale` + `decided_at` | The human judgment and when it occurred |

The KLA Digital framework specifies: "Bind approval to actor + action + target + destination + parameters + source version + limit + time window. If a material field changes, require a new approval."

If policy required approval and none exists, or if `decided_at > executed_at`, the side effect is unapproved regardless of the outcome.

**Source:** KLA Digital, "How to Audit an AI Agent System," Jul 2026. https://kla.digital/blog/how-to-audit-ai-agent-system

### 4. Tool Execution and Effect Record

**Question answered:** What actually happened in the target system?

This is the only artifact that captures whether a side effect occurred. Critical fields:

- `tool_id` + `tool_version` — what capability was invoked
- `action` + `resource_scope` — what operation on which target
- `before_state_hash` + `after_state_hash` — **evidence of change**
- `gateway_ref` — link back to the authorizing policy decision
- `execution_status` — succeeded / failed / denied

The `before_state_hash` and `after_state_hash` are what distinguish a side effect that occurred from one that was merely proposed. The PoE paper's Invariant I3 (Null effect on deny) requires that for any deny decision, all causal descendants have `effect_type = none` and `delta_hash = ⊥`. A deny verdict followed by `after_state_hash ≠ before_state_hash` is a direct signal of an unapproved side effect.

### 5. Integrity and Ordering Envelope

**Question answered:** Can the evidence be trusted?

Every event must be hash-chained (`prev_event_hash`), signed (Ed25519 or ECDSA P-256), and carry a monotonic `commit_seq` and `sealed_at` timestamp. The IETF SCITT AI Agent Action Receipt Profile (draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00) requires: "Each receipt carries a monotonic `chain.seq` and `prevHash`. A verifier checking a range of receipts MUST verify each `prevHash` equals the prior receipt's `chain.hash`."

Without this envelope, evidence can be mutated post hoc, erasing the distinction between approved and unapproved.

**Source:** IETF, "SCITT AI Agent Action Receipt Profile." https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00

## The Differentiator: Temporal Ordering

The single most diagnostic piece of evidence is the **provable temporal sequence**:

```
Policy Evaluation → (Approval, if required) → Execution → Effect Verification
```

An **approved** side effect satisfies: `evaluated_at < any decided_at < executed_at < sealed_at`, with all records linked by correlation identifiers.

An **unapproved** side effect manifests as any of these six patterns:

| Pattern | Evidence Signal |
|---------|----------------|
| Missing policy decision | No `policy_decision` record exists for the action |
| Post-hoc policy evaluation | `evaluated_at > executed_at` |
| Deny-with-effect | `policy_decision = deny` but `after_state_hash ≠ before_state_hash` (PoE I3 violation) |
| Scope exceedance | `authorized_scope` does not contain the executed `capability` |
| Missing required approval | Policy returned `Step-Up` but no `decision_request_id` exists |
| Gateway bypass | No `gateway_ref` links the effect to an authorization decision |

## The Five Dimensions Framework

Zhao et al. (2026) establish that a defensible audit verdict requires five jointly necessary dimensions:

1. **Action Recoverability** — What happened? Tool, arguments, output, timestamp, policy decision.
2. **Lifecycle Coverage** — Were all phases recorded? Allow, block, pending, approval states; delegation chains; retries.
3. **Policy Checkability** — Was the action mechanically checked against policy? Policy version, matched rules, decision.
4. **Responsibility Attribution** — Who is responsible? Agent identity, session context, delegation chain, human principal.
5. **Evidence Integrity** — Can the record be trusted? Ed25519 signatures, SHA-256 hash-chaining.

If any dimension is unsatisfied, the system cannot reliably distinguish approved from unapproved side effects. The framework's Auditability Card (Q1–Q6) provides a standardized disclosure format for evaluating whether a deployed system meets these requirements.

**Source:** Zhao et al., "Auditable Agents," ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026. https://viterbi-web.usc.edu/~yzhao010/auditable-agents.html

## Architecture Requirements for Producing Distinguishing Evidence

Four structural requirements emerge across the literature:

1. **Mandatory Gateway / PEP.** All effectful actions must pass through a single authoritative enforcement point. The PoE paper's Prime Execution Model separates Planning, Enforcement, Effect, and Recordkeeping into distinct authority planes. OATS enforces this at compile time via typestates.

2. **Append-Only, Tamper-Evident Journal.** Records must be hash-chained and signed. Aegis, Tracepath, OATS, and the SCITT profiles converge on this.

3. **Independent Evidence Custodian.** The IETF SCITT profiles recommend evidence be registered with an independent Transparency Service to prevent operator-side mutation.

4. **Coordinator-Specialist Topology.** For multi-agent systems, all interactions must pass through a mandatory logging point. Peer-to-peer topologies lack structural traceability.

## Practical Litmus Test

CellCog's reconstruction standard provides the operational test:

> "Starting from one business outcome or one disputed action, can a reviewer move backward to its origin (identity → delegation → policy decision → approval) and forward to every material consequence (tool execution → state change → verification → acceptance)?"

If the answer is no, the evidence is insufficient to classify that side effect as approved or unapproved.

**Source:** CellCog, "AI Employee Audit Logs," Jul 2026. https://cellcog.ai/blog/ai-employee-audit-logs/

## Evidence That Is Insufficient Alone

| Record Type | Why It Cannot Distinguish Approved from Unapproved |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Tool-call traces | No authorization context, no policy version, no principal |
| Application logs | No delegation chain, no approval binding |
| Model transcripts | Unreliable as evidence; no authorization proof |
| API access logs | No decision context, no before/after state |
| OpenTelemetry spans | No authorization semantics, no policy identity |

As WorkOS notes: "The application log tells you what happened. The audit log tells you who did it, what they were allowed to do, who authorized the action, and how long that authorization was valid for." (WorkOS, "Why AI Agent Audit Logs Are Different from Application Logs," Jun 2026. https://workos.com/blog/agent-audit-logs)

---

## Limitations

- No final, agent-specific, end-to-end auditability standard exists as of the source review. NIST AI RMF 1.0 and ISO/IEC 42001 provide organizational frameworks but lack agent-specific control detail.
- The IETF SCITT-based profiles are Internet-Drafts, not RFCs.
- OATS is an open specification, not a ratified standard; conformance is testable but not independently certified.
- The PoE paper is an academic publication; soundness depends on deployment assumptions (A3–A7) that must be validated per implementation.
- No framework claims to detect side effects occurring entirely outside the instrumented boundary — the evidence gap itself is the signal.

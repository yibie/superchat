# Audit Evidence to Distinguish Approved from Unapproved External Side Effects in Agent Systems — Reviewer Assessment

## Research Question
Which audit evidence is needed to distinguish an approved external side effect from an unapproved one in an agent system?

## Reviewer Verdict
The collector and analyst artifacts are substantially accurate and well-sourced. All primary sources are accessible and their claims verified. The core answer is consistent across sources: five jointly necessary evidence categories, linked by causal identifiers and protected by cryptographic integrity, with **provable temporal ordering (Policy → Approval → Execution → Verification)** as the decisive differentiator. No material contradictions between sources were found. Below is the refined, reviewed answer incorporating source verification notes, corrections, and a sharper distinction between evidence and inference.

---

## 1. The Distinguishing Evidence: Five Jointly Necessary Categories

An external side effect can be classified as "approved" only when a causally ordered, tamper-evident evidence chain exists establishing that a policy decision demonstrably preceded the effect, and the effect fell within the authorized scope. Five evidence categories are jointly necessary:

### 1.1 Identity and Delegation Chain

**Audit question:** Who is accountable for this action?

| Minimum Field | Role in Distinction |
|---------------|---------------------|
| `agent_id` | The executing agent, distinct from any human user |
| `sponsoring_principal_id` | The accountable human or service principal |
| `delegation_chain` | Ordered authority path from principal to executor |
| `session_id` | Authorized session scope |
| `authority_snapshot_id` | Effective grants at execution time |

**Source basis:** NIST NCCoE concept paper (Feb 2026) warns: "Treating an AI agent as a delegated user collapses accountability by merging agent actions into the identity record of the human principal it serves." Without a distinct agent identity and delegation chain, there is no accountable principal — the side effect is unapproved by definition. Zhao et al. (2026) treat Responsibility Attribution as a jointly necessary dimension; CellCog (2026) requires three identity layers (initiating principal, executing identity, accountable owner). [Verified accessible; NIST NCCoE is a draft concept paper — cite as work-in-progress guidance, not final standard.]

### 1.2 Pre-Execution Policy Decision Record

**Audit question:** Was this specific action mechanically evaluated against policy before execution?

| Minimum Field | Role in Distinction |
|---------------|---------------------|
| `policy_id` + `policy_version` | Exact policy ruleset in force |
| `policy_decision` | allow / deny / require_approval / modify / block |
| `matched_rule_ids[]` | Which specific rules fired |
| `evaluated_at` | Timestamp proving evaluation occurred before execution |

**Source basis:** OATS specification (C3): "The runtime MUST evaluate actions against policy before execution. Default MUST be deny." The PoE paper (Rhodes & Kang, arXiv:2607.05397) formalizes this as Invariant I2: every effectful event must have a unique Gateway allow decision `g` such that `g` precedes `e` in causal order and `g.authorized_scope(e.capability) = 1`. Permit.io (Jun 2026): "Authorization decisions should be recorded as first-class events before protected tool execution." [All sources verified accessible. PoE paper is an academic preprint with preliminary prototype; soundness depends on deployment assumptions A3–A7 that must be validated per implementation.]

### 1.3 Human Approval Record (Conditional)

**Audit question:** When policy required human judgment, was it exercised before the effect?

Required only when policy returns `Step-Up` / `require_approval` or the action exceeds an autonomous risk threshold.

| Minimum Field | Role in Distinction |
|---------------|---------------------|
| `decision_request_id` | Links approval to the specific action proposal |
| `reviewer_id` + `reviewer_role` | Who reviewed, under what authority |
| `presented_evidence_hash` | Content the reviewer saw — prevents approval drift |
| `decision` + `rationale` + `decided_at` | Judgment and when it occurred |

**Source basis:** KLA Digital framework (Jul 2026): "Bind approval to actor + action + target + destination + parameters + source version + limit + time window. If a material field changes, require a new approval." CellCog (2026): if `decided_at > executed_at`, the side effect is unapproved regardless of outcome. [Both verified accessible.]

### 1.4 Tool Execution and Effect Record

**Audit question:** What actually changed in the external system?

| Minimum Field | Role in Distinction |
|---------------|---------------------|
| `tool_id` + `tool_version` | What capability was invoked |
| `action` + `resource_scope` | What operation, on which target |
| `before_state_hash` + `after_state_hash` | Evidence of what changed (the side effect itself) |
| `gateway_ref` | Link back to the authorizing policy decision |
| `execution_status` | succeeded / failed / unknown / denied |

**Source basis:** PoE paper Invariant I3 (Null effect on deny): for any deny decision, all causal descendants must have `effect_type = none` and `delta_hash = ⊥`. A deny verdict followed by `after_state_hash ≠ before_state_hash` is a direct signal of an unapproved side effect. KLA Digital: "Reconcile recorded outcome with the business system." [Verified accessible.]

### 1.5 Integrity and Ordering Envelope

**Audit question:** Can the evidence be trusted — and in what order did events occur?

| Minimum Field | Role in Distinction |
|---------------|---------------------|
| `evidence_hash` | Content hash of the complete record |
| `previous_record_hash` | Hash-chain link to prior event |
| `signature` + `signature_key_id` | Cryptographic non-repudiation |
| `sealed_at` + `commit_seq` | Monotonic ordering proof |

**Source basis:** IETF draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-00: "Each receipt carries a monotonic `chain.seq` and `prevHash`. A verifier checking a range of receipts MUST verify each `prevHash` equals the prior receipt's `chain.hash`." OATS Section 9: hash-chained journals with Ed25519 signatures. Microsoft AGT: Merkle audit chain with inclusion proofs. [IETF drafts verified accessible; all are Internet-Drafts, not RFCs — cite as work-in-progress. OATS is an open specification, not a ratified standard.]

## 2. The Decisive Differentiator: Provable Temporal Ordering

The single most diagnostic piece of evidence is the **provable temporal sequence**:

```
Policy Evaluation → (Approval, if required) → Execution → Effect Verification
```

An **approved** side effect satisfies: `evaluated_at < any decided_at < executed_at < sealed_at`, with all records linked by correlation identifiers and gateway references.

An **unapproved** side effect manifests as one of six detectable patterns:

| Pattern | Evidence Signal |
|---------|----------------|
| Missing policy decision | No `policy_decision` record exists for the action |
| Post-hoc evaluation | `evaluated_at > executed_at` |
| Deny-with-effect | `policy_decision = deny` but `after_state_hash ≠ before_state_hash` (PoE I3 violation) |
| Scope exceedance | `authorized_scope` does not contain the executed `capability` |
| Missing required approval | Policy required `Step-Up` but no `decision_request_id` exists |
| Gateway bypass | No `gateway_ref` links the effect to an authorization decision |

**All six patterns are inferences from the evidence framework, not independently validated empirical claims.** The PoE paper provides the formalization (theorem-proven under explicit assumptions). The OATS specification defines the structural conditions. No source provides empirical measurement of how frequently each pattern occurs in deployed systems.

## 3. The Five Dimensions Framework (Zhao et al. 2026)

Zhao et al. establish that a defensible audit verdict requires five jointly necessary dimensions: Action Recoverability, Lifecycle Coverage, Policy Checkability, Responsibility Attribution, and Evidence Integrity. If any dimension is unsatisfied, the system cannot reliably distinguish approved from unapproved side effects. [Source verified accessible: https://viterbi-web.usc.edu/~yzhao010/auditable-agents.html ; paper accepted to ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026, also on arXiv:2604.05485.]

---

## 4. Evidence That Is Insufficient Alone

Several common data sources are necessary for operational observability but cannot independently distinguish approved from unapproved side effects:

- **Tool-call traces:** No authorization context, policy version, or principal identity
- **Application logs:** No delegation chain or approval binding
- **Model transcripts:** Unreliable as evidence; no authorization proof
- **API access logs:** No decision context or before/after state
- **OpenTelemetry spans:** No authorization semantics or policy identity

WorkOS (Jun 2026): "The application log tells you what happened. The audit log tells you who did it, what they were allowed to do, who authorized the action, and how long that authorization was valid for." [Verified accessible.]

---

## 5. Architecture Requirements for Producing Distinguishing Evidence

Four structural requirements emerge across the literature:

1. **Mandatory Gateway / Policy Enforcement Point:** All effectful actions must pass through a single authoritative enforcement point. OATS enforces this at compile time via typestates; PoE separates planning, enforcement, effect, and recordkeeping into distinct authority planes.

2. **Append-Only, Tamper-Evident Journal:** Records must be hash-chained and signed. Aegis, the IETF SCITT profiles, OATS, and Microsoft AGT all converge on this pattern.

3. **Independent Evidence Custodian:** IETF SCITT profiles recommend evidence be registered with an independent Transparency Service to prevent operator-side mutation. draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00: "Operator-controlled audit logs are mutable by the operator, carry no third-party attestation, and are not designed for independent verification."

4. **Coordinator-Specialist Topology (multi-agent):** All interactions pass through a mandatory logging point. AugmentCode (Apr 2026): peer-to-peer topologies lack structural traceability. [Verified accessible.]

---

## 6. Practical Litmus Test

CellCog (Jul 2026): "Starting from one business outcome or one disputed action, can a reviewer move backward to its origin (identity → delegation → policy decision → approval) and forward to every material consequence (tool execution → state change → verification → acceptance)?" If the answer is no, the evidence is insufficient. [Verified accessible.]

## 8. Distinction of Evidence from Inference

| Claim | Classification | Basis |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Five evidence categories are jointly necessary | **Inference** | Zhao et al. argue joint necessity; PoE proves invariant independence; no empirical study confirms joint necessity in deployed systems |
| Temporal ordering is the decisive differentiator | **Inference** | Logically grounded in all sources; PoE formalizes it; no empirical measurement of detection rates |
| Six unapproved-side-effect patterns | **Inference** | Derived from combining PoE invariants with OATS decision states; not empirically validated |
| Hash-chaining + signatures provide tamper-evidence | **Evidence** | Cryptographic property; well-established in literature beyond agent systems |
| Policy decision MUST precede execution | **Evidence** | Normative requirement in OATS C3, PoE I2, Permit.io; stated as MUST/SHALL in specifications |
| No final agent-specific auditability standard exists | **Evidence** | KLA Digital explicitly states this; NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative is a roadmap; IETF drafts are not RFCs |

---

## 9. Limitations

1. **No finalized agent-specific standard exists as of the source review.** NIST AI RMF 1.0 and ISO/IEC 42001 provide organizational frameworks but lack agent-specific control detail. The NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative is a roadmap. The IETF SCITT-based profiles are Internet-Drafts.

2. **The PoE paper is an academic preprint.** Its formal soundness proof depends on deployment assumptions (A3–A7) that must be validated per implementation. The prototype reports ~2.7 ms overhead on a single-node setup.

3. **OATS is an open specification, not a ratified standard.** Its conformance requirements are testable but not certified by any standards body. Five of seven core requirements have initial empirical validation.

4. **No framework claims to detect side effects occurring entirely outside the instrumented boundary.** If an agent bypasses the Gateway/PEP entirely, the evidence gap itself is the only signal. This is acknowledged by PoE (A5/trace completeness), OATS (Section 5.5), and the NIST NCCoE concept paper.

5. **Several cited sources are vendor-published guidance** (KLA Digital, CellCog, Permit.io, WorkOS). Their frameworks are practical and internally consistent but reflect commercial perspectives. Their convergence with academic and standards-track sources strengthens rather than weakens the overall answer.

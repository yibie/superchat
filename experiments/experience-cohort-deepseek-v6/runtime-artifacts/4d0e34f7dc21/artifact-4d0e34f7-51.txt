# Reviewer Artifact: Conditions for Deterministic Replay in Event-Sourced Systems Under Schema Evolution

## Research Question

What conditions are required for deterministic replay in event-sourced systems when schemas evolve?

## Prior Artifacts Reviewed

- **Collector (artifact-4d0e34f7-49):** Comprehensive 13-condition enumeration sourced from Akka specifications, Gregory Young, Overeem et al. (2021), and multiple framework docs. Well-structured and thoroughly referenced.
- **Analyst (artifact-4d0e34f7-50):** Evaluated evidence quality per condition, distinguished necessary vs. sufficient conditions, identified gaps around semantic drift, and flagged the tension between the Akka upcasting assumption and Young's weak-schema preference. Sharp, balanced analysis.

## Verified Source Base

The following sources were independently re-read and their key claims confirmed:

1. **Akka Data Integrity Exit Conditions** — directly verified. All C1–C13 conditions are explicitly stated as testable exit criteria. https://doc.akka.io/reference/specify/catalog/data-integrity.html

2. **Gregory Young, _Versioning in an Event Sourced System_** (Leanpub) — the full text is gated behind purchase, but the table of contents and available excerpts confirm the topics covered (weak schema, double publish, copy-and-replace, upcasting, negotiation). The key claim "a new version must be convertible from the old version, or it is a new event" is widely corroborated in secondary sources. https://leanpub.com/esversioning/read

3. **Overeem et al. (2021), JSS** — directly verified. Peer-reviewed grounded theory study of 19 systems, 25 engineers. Confirms: five schema evolution tactics (versioned events, weak schema, upcasting, in-place transformation, copy-and-transform); three degrees of immutability (strict: 8/19, cut-off moments: 3/19, mutable: 8/19). Advises starting with versioned events + weak schema, evolving to upcasting and copy-and-transform. https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.01146

4. **Protean Event Versioning and Evolution** — directly verified. Golden rules confirmed: new fields get defaults, old fields never removed, semantics never change, breaking changes create new event types. `renamed_from` mechanism, upcaster chains with gap/cycle detection, `lenient_deserialization` escape hatch. https://docs.proteanhq.com/patterns/event-versioning-and-evolution/

5. **NILUS Event Replay Isolation** (2025) — directly verified. Key contribution: distinguishes replay as "candidate truth" requiring reconciliation, identifies semantic mistranslation as the most dangerous failure mode ("looks healthy, produces wrong answers"), and introduces semantic classes of replay (projection, decision-model, migration). https://www.nilus.be/blog/event_replay_isolation_in_event-sourced_systems/

6. **Akbulut (2026), Medium** — directly verified. Argues that semantic drift is "the most expensive form of software debt" in event sourcing, and that "unknown" ≠ "default" for historical fields. Introduces concept of event-store profiling + consumer analysis + runtime telemetry for evidence-based migration recommendations. https://medium.com/@umutt.akbulut/making-event-sourcing-more-productive-automatic-migration-recommendations-in-event-schema-5a75fc30f564

## Critical Gap Identified: Semantic Drift

Both prior artifacts acknowledge semantic drift but underrepresent its severity. My direct source review reveals this is the **most dangerous gap in the condition set**:

- The Akka conditions guarantee **syntactic determinism** (byte-identical state), not semantic correctness.
- NILUS (2025): "Events are successfully upcast at the schema level but mapped incorrectly in domain meaning. The system runs cleanly and produces wrong answers. This is the most dangerous failure because it looks healthy."
- Akbulut (2026): "Semantic drift... becomes one of the most expensive forms of software debt — because automated tests often won't catch it. Tests validate today, but the break happens when the past is read with today's rules."
- Gregory Young's book includes a chapter "Changing Semantic Meaning" (confirmed via TOC), treating it as a distinct concern from schema versioning.

**A 14th condition is warranted:** replay must either (a) preserve the original semantics under which events were written by applying temporally correct business rules and reference data, or (b) explicitly declare and reconcile semantic divergence as "candidate truth" (NILUS reconciliation pattern). Without this, deterministic replay can silently succeed while producing semantically wrong results.

## Condition Taxonomy (Revised)

I propose organizing the conditions into three tiers:

### Tier 1: Universal Necessary Conditions (apply regardless of strategy)

1. Pure event handlers (no I/O, no side effects)
2. No wall-clock time or randomness in state transitions
3. Events immutable after persist
4. Events replayed in original sequence order
5. Every event type has a corresponding handler
6. Old events remain deserializable under the current schema
7. No incompatible type changes without a bridging mechanism

### Tier 2: Strategy-Dependent Conditions

8. **If using upcasting:** upcaster coverage for every historical version + version tagging on events
9. **If using weak schema:** tolerant reader handling (missing fields → defaults, unknown fields → ignored)
10. **If using snapshots:** snapshot backward compatibility with evolved schemas
11. **If using projections:** idempotent updates with durable offset tracking

### Tier 3: Semantic Correctness Condition (underrepresented in specifications)

12. **Semantic fidelity:** replay must manage reference data temporality (historical vs. current), business rule versioning, and domain meaning evolution. Where semantic fidelity cannot be guaranteed, replay output must be treated as "candidate truth" requiring reconciliation against production state.

## Empirical Reality vs. Formal Conditions

Overeem et al. (2021) found that 8 of 19 production ESSs allow mutable events and 3 of 19 permit "cut-off moments" where events change under backup protection. Only 8 of 19 enforce strict immutability. This means roughly half of real-world systems violate C3 (event immutability), making strict deterministic replay as specified by Akka unachievable in those systems. The formal conditions represent an ideal; practitioners often accept weaker guarantees as a tradeoff for operational flexibility. The NILUS replay isolation pattern represents a pragmatic middle ground: accept that the world has moved on, isolate replay from production, and reconcile differences explicitly.

## Conclusion

The collector's 13 conditions are well-sourced and substantially accurate. The analyst correctly identifies that C6 and C11 are design guidance rather than strict necessary conditions, that C8 and C9 are upcasting-dependent, and that semantic drift is an underexplored gap. The reviewer confirms these assessments through independent source verification and proposes: (1) a 14th condition covering semantic fidelity / temporal reference data management, (2) a three-tier taxonomy distinguishing universal, strategy-dependent, and semantic-correctness conditions, and (3) acknowledgment that the gap between formal conditions and empirical practice (Overeem's immutability findings) is significant and should inform any practical adoption of deterministic replay guarantees.
